CampusLink

Making campus life more connected.

CampusLink is a campus-focused platform I'm building to make university life easier, more connected, and more engaging.

University life involves much more than attending lectures and writing examinations. Students look for opportunities, join communities, attend events, work on projects, buy and sell things, and try to keep up with everything happening around campus. Unfortunately, a lot of this information is scattered across WhatsApp groups, social media pages, notice boards, and word of mouth.

I believe there should be a better way to bring these experiences together.

That's the idea behind CampusLink.

Why I'm Building This

The idea started from observing some of the everyday challenges students face around campus.

Finding out about a tech event, discovering a student community, hearing about an opportunity, locating a lost item, or even knowing what's happening around the university can sometimes be more difficult than it should be.

Important information gets lost, students miss opportunities, and communities struggle to reach the people who might benefit from what they offer.

I want CampusLink to help change that.

I'm starting with the challenges I understand around my university, Federal University Dutsin-Ma (FUDMA), but the bigger vision is to build something that other universities can also adopt.

What I'm Building

CampusLink is being developed with different aspects of campus life in mind.

Some of the areas I want to bring together include:

- Campus Activities & Events: Discover upcoming events, workshops, hackathons, seminars, and other activities.
- Communities: Find student communities, connect with people who share similar interests, and participate in collaborative activities.
- Projects: Discover projects, find collaborators, and work with other students to build useful things.
- Opportunities: Stay informed about internships, scholarships, competitions, fellowships, and other opportunities.
- Campus Services: Access useful campus-related services and information.
- Student Directory: Make it easier for students to discover and connect with one another.
- Lost & Found: Help students report and recover lost belongings.
- Venue Management: Make it easier to check venue availability and reduce conflicts when scheduling lectures and other activities.

These are part of the larger vision. Features will be introduced and improved as development progresses.

Technology

I'm building CampusLink with Microsoft's .NET ecosystem.

Current technology choices include:

- C# – Main programming language.
- .NET 10 – Application framework.
- Blazor – User interface and interactive web experience.
- Entity Framework Core – Database access and data management.
- SQLite – Initial database choice.

The project is an opportunity for me to deepen my software engineering skills while working on a problem that has real relevance to my environment.

Development Status

CampusLink is currently under active development.

I'm building it step by step, starting with the core website experience and gradually working towards the broader platform.

Not every feature described here is available yet. Some are planned, while others are still being designed.

I believe in building something useful, testing it, learning from the process, and improving it over time.

The Bigger Picture

I don't want CampusLink to become just another university website.

I want it to become a useful digital space where students can discover opportunities, connect with communities, collaborate on projects, and stay informed about what's happening around them.

Starting at FUDMA, I hope to develop a platform that can eventually serve other universities as well.

There is a lot to build, and this is just the beginning.

Getting Started

If you'd like to run the project locally:

1. Install the ".NET 10 SDK" (https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download/dotnet/10.0).

2. Clone the repository:
   
   git clone https://github.com/gidadomukhtar/CampusLink.git

3. Navigate into the project directory:
   
   cd CampusLink

4. Run the application:
   
   dotnet run

5. Open the local URL displayed in your terminal.
